## Why

These are pending game rows (ledger class `none`, `partial_native` or `candidate_port`; region `game`) that come in identical instruction shapes (all numbers masked): one fixture and one C implementation per shape, constants per row from that row's own disassembly. A survey of the 1,234 rows left after FL-36's 533 found 68 shapes with at least two members. FL-36 (unmerged branch `claude/project-thread-ceq3bv`) owns every get-constant, empty, constructor, destructor, setter and forwarder row; this packet takes only rows that FL-36 does not touch, and the lists below were taken after removing its 533 rows.

## Bar (unchanged)

A row is ported only when the original-only oracle (`tests/fl38<x>_original_oracle.py`, Unicorn over the original bytes, callees and vtable entries as recording stubs at the call boundary) executes N/N instructions and takes both directions of every conditional jump, the C port matches it in the direct test and the Unicorn differential test, and mutants are caught (use `tests/fl36_mutation_check.py` as the template: 0 survivors). Any row with an untaken jump direction, an unreachable block, x87 or an indirect jump stays pending and goes into the handoff with the disassembly reason. An exception path that never returns (a callee that ends the program) is modelled as in the earlier fixtures that have a `fault`/`partial` mechanism (`fl28b_fixtures.py`); if a row cannot be driven that way, drop it and say why. Never assume a family member equals its neighbour: assert the whole masked instruction stream of every row and read every constant from that row.
